The harbor was quiet, the village asleep,
When the great wooden ship sailed out into the deep.
The lanterns were dimmed and the sails were unfurled,
To chase the horizon at the edge of the world.
With a compass of brass and a map made of hide,
The crew placed their trust in the wind and the tide.
For weeks they saw nothing but endless blue grey,
As the foam of the ocean washed over the spray.
They battled the gales where the storm-demons roar,
And prayed for the sight of a welcoming shore.
Then out of the mist on the twenty-third dawn,
The shadows of mountains began to be drawn.
A shoreline of emerald, untouched and grand,
Spoke peace to the travelers who longed for the land.
They dropped the heavy anchor inside a calm bay,
And stepped onto beaches where wild creatures play.
They marched through the jungles where ancient trees stand,
Like giant green pillars commanding the land.
The vines hung like ropes from the canopy high,
Where birds of bright ruby and sapphire flew by.
They climbed up the ridges through rivers of stone,
To places where footsteps had never been known.
And there, at the peak where the fierce winds blow free,
They looked down in wonder at valley and sea.
Deep in the canyons where shadows run deep,
They found an old city forgotten in sleep.
The walls were of marble, o'ergrown by the weed,
With symbols and secrets that no one could read.
A fountain of water, still crystal and clear,
Had flowed through the silence for year after year.
It proved that a kingdom had once flourished there,
Before it vanished like mist in the air.
The sun started sinking, a brilliant deep red,
As blankets of stars were pulled over their head.
The campfires were lit and the stories were told,
Of all of the wonders they’d lived to behold.
They knew they would leave when the morning grew bright,
To carry these tales back into the night.
For the greatest of treasures a journey can yield,
Is the wonder of worlds that are newly revealed.
