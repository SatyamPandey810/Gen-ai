Arthur found a brass key inside a loaf of sliced bread. It was sticky with yeast and slightly warm, as if it had just come out of a bakery oven. He wiped it on his trousers and stared at it. He lived on the fourth floor of an old brick building, and he did not own anything that used a brass key.Later that afternoon, Arthur tried the key on the communal laundry room door. It did not fit. He tried it on the basement padlock. It was too small. Just as he was about to drop it into the trash can, he noticed a tiny, matching keyhole hidden behind the baseboard in his own kitchen. He knelt down, inserted the key, and turned it. With a soft click, a small section of the wall slid open to reveal a narrow, dusty chute and a folded note that simply read: “Do not feed the pigeons after midnight.”
